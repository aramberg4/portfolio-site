Top 10 Mirror is the algo carrying the whole experiment: rank Polymarket wallets by lifetime PnL, filter out the bots, mirror the top ten. Its v3/v4 rebuild has been the best-supported strategy on the board — a big settled sample, a real profit factor, and a 27% drawdown we described three weeks ago as "the price of admission."

This weekend it paid admission again, and this time the mechanism wasn't variance. It was the roster itself. The algo closed Friday, August 21 at $14,064 — a whisker off its all-time high. By Sunday morning it printed $9,512, a 33% fall from Saturday's intraday peak, and it sits around $10,261 now: a month of gains, back to roughly the $10,000 it started with.

Friday, 23:16 UTC: the roster refresh seats a stranger

Every six hours the algo re-ranks the leaderboard and refreshes its ten seats. At the Friday-night refresh it seated a wallet named Djdjdjekekek — and yes, the name is exactly the kind of keyboard mash you'd expect from an account created in a hurry. Our own tracking had first noticed the wallet 48 days earlier. In those 48 days it had made 68 tracked buys totaling $5.1 million — average clip about $75,000, average price 56¢, all of it directional sports and esports betting. No arbitrage, no market-making, no hedging. From the bot gate's point of view, a model citizen: exactly the genuine directional bettor the v3 rebuild was built to find.

Over the next thirty hours the mirror copied it 17 times across 83 fills: Dota 2's The International, the Cincinnati Open, MLB, NFL preseason. Total deployed: $5,799 — about 41% of the book's equity — behind one gambler's weekend.

The weekend went how weekends go. Iga Swiatek lost at 67¢. The Eagles lost at 83¢. The Rays lost at 56¢. And in the middle of it, the wallet did the single most human thing in this whole story: mid-way through a Dota 2 series it had bet on Team Spirit at 70¢, it flipped, and bought Team Yandex four separate times at 37–44¢ as the series ran. The mirror faithfully copied all five legs of that chase. The event cost us −$1,004 on its own.

Why every gate waved it through

Here's the uncomfortable part: nothing malfunctioned. The wallet passed every check we have, honestly.

The roster ranks by lifetime PnL — that was the entire point of the v3 rebuild, "proven long-run edge" instead of the 30-day boards' streak-chasers. The liveness floor (20+ trades in 30 days) was trivially cleared by a wallet making 68 large bets in seven weeks. The bot gate, which measures matched-notional arbitrage share, read a pure directional gambler and correctly said not a bot. And the catastrophic-loss cap — reject any wallet whose 30-day loss exceeds a quarter of its lifetime PnL — did nothing, because the wallet had no 30-day loss. It was on a heater.

Root causeFor a 48-day-old wallet, lifetime PnL is the hot streak. The ranking key cannot distinguish six weeks of variance from six years of edge, and the loss cap compares two numbers that are, for a young winner, the same number. Every gate we built assumes a history long enough to have a shape. This wallet didn't have a history; it had a moment.

We can't even claim surprise. When v4 shipped on August 17, we flagged a month-old wallet named 3edmond.dantes that had ranked onto the board with lifetime PnL exactly equal to its 30-day PnL, wrote "minimum wallet-age seat requirement" on the list of parked decisions, and scheduled the review for August 24. The failure mode beat the meeting by two days.

What held: the rookie cap earned its keep

One v4 rule did exactly what it was designed to do. Seats less than seven days old trade at a 5% per-market cap instead of the veteran 10%, and every Djdjdjekekek position was clipped at it — the biggest single position was $628 on a ~$12,600 book. At veteran sizing, the same slate loses roughly twice as much. The plumbing held up too: when the wallet later fell off the roster, the mirror kept watching it for exits on the positions still open, which is exactly the behavior v2 famously lacked.

What workedRookie sizing halved the damage, and off-roster exit tracking worked as designed. The failure was concentration and selection, not execution.

But a per-market cap is not a per-wallet cap. Seventeen concurrent positions at 5% each still stacked 41% of equity behind one bettor's judgment on one weekend's slate. The sizing rules were written to stop a single market from sinking the book. Nothing in them contemplates a single person doing it seventeen markets at a time.

The roster is a momentum trade we never meant to make

The refresh log makes the structural problem legible. Between August 18 and August 23 the roster updated 21 times with 44 seat changes — about eight swaps a day on a ten-seat roster. The boundary between seat #10 and the field is noisy, so wallets churn constantly at the margin.

And the churn has a direction. A wallet crosses the boundary at the exact moment its cumulative PnL peaks — that's what crossing the boundary means — so the mirror systematically starts copying bettors at the top of their streaks and drops them after the reversion. It is, structurally, a buy-high-sell-low machine for seat selection. Djdjdjekekek is the cartoon version: seated Friday 23:16 UTC at the peak of the heater, dropped Sunday 05:16 after the losses landed…

…and re-seated Sunday at 17:16, twelve hours later, because its lifetime PnL still ranks. As we publish this, Djdjdjekekek holds a seat on the live roster — alongside 3edmond.dantes, the very wallet that prompted the parked wallet-age rule. Next weekend's slate is fully capable of replaying this post.

What ships before next weekend

1 · Seasoning gate

A seat requires a trading history old enough to mean something — first tracked activity at least ~90 days back, using the same activity page the vetting already fetches. Newcomers on heaters wait out the streak test; five-year grinders are unaffected.

2 · Per-seat exposure cap

Cap total open cost per source wallet at ~15% of equity (rookies lower). Turns a bad seat from −$2,000 into roughly −$700, no matter how busy its weekend is.

3 · Event cap, extended to the mirror

The per-event concentration cap that shipped in the day-33 overhaul only guards the classified stream — the mirror never passes an event slug. Plumb it through, so five legs of one Dota series count as one bet.

4 · Roster hysteresis

A candidate must rank in-seat for two or three consecutive refreshes before being seated. Cuts the eight-swaps-a-day churn and stops the mirror from buying every streak at its exact top.

5 · Keep the rookie cap

Unchanged. It's the one control that demonstrably reduced this loss, and hysteresis plus seasoning make its seven-day clock meaningful instead of resettable.

The Inverse Losers post-mortem taught us what happens when the prescriptions don't ship — the algo rebuilt the exact book that blew it up, one Fed meeting later. This time the review was already on the calendar for August 24. The incident just moved it up a day.
